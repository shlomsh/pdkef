# SIGN-19 · Four shaping/render guards passed on macOS and failed on Linux CI

## Scope and acceptance

**Four shaping/render guards passed on macOS and failed on Linux CI.** **Resolved. The premise the three options in the brief shared was false: it is not one rasteriser noise per platform, it is two artefacts with different causes, and separating them let one be deleted rather than calibrated around.** **Artefact 1, glyph rasteriser mismatch:** `fillText` draws Skia's cached glyph *bitmaps* while the reconstruction fills outlines through `Path2D`, so they disagree along every antialiased edge - dominant on macOS (Arabic floor 14.89%), minor on Linux (2.76%). **Removed** by rendering above Skia's ~256px bitmap-glyph limit, where `fillText` itself rasterises via paths: the floor collapses to 0.00% on both platforms, so **the Arabic tolerance falls from 22.33% to 3.00% on macOS, a 7x tightening, not a loosening**. The threshold was located rather than guessed - Scheherazade New is still noisy at 240px (5.79%) and clean at 320px, Noto Sans Bengali is already clean at 300px. **Artefact 2, advance quantisation:** Linux Chromium reports whole-pixel `measureText` advances (FreeType hinting) where macOS matches fontkit to floating point (measured: widthDiff 0.00 on 151/151 Arabic cases; Linux integral on 40/40 sampled). The error is per glyph and **accumulates**, which a single-glyph calibration set structurally cannot see - measured on zero-ambiguity ink at the old size, Linux ran 1.35% at one glyph to 17.13% at seventeen against a calibrated floor of 2.76%. It is **not removable**: it survives every render size and every configuration tried (`--enable-font-subpixel-positioning`, `deviceScaleFactor: 2`, `--force-device-scale-factor=2`). So it is **measured** instead - `measureDisplacementFloorPct` renders each corpus string's reconstruction twice from the *same* fontkit output, once at fontkit's exact positions and once with every advance rounded to a whole pixel, and diffs those two against each other. The native rendering is never involved, so it can be measured over the corpus without circularity, and it reads zero on a platform that does not quantise, so macOS pays nothing. **Result: Arabic/Pashto/Bengali give the same verdict on both platforms** (macOS tolerance 3.00%/3.00%/4.00%, Linux 7.61%/9.83%/4.00%, 0 failing everywhere). Power is unchanged: the sabotage control (shape every character in isolation, i.e. no joining) failed 119/151 Arabic at the old 22.33% tolerance, so it fails at least that many at every tolerance now in force. **The brief's own "prefer the tighter floor" rule was wrong and is replaced.** Applied here it would have picked Linux's 2.76% over macOS's 14.89%, and Linux's was the *less* trustworthy number, because it was measured on ink carrying none of the artefact that dominates the cases under test. The replacement, now in the harness: **an artefact gets removed from the instrument if it can be, and measured if it cannot; it never gets absorbed into a hand-picked tolerance.** **The export render guard is a genuinely different problem and did take Option 1.** Determinism is 0.00% on both platforms, so `signPdf` is byte-identical and only pdf.js's rasterisation of two thin handwriting faces differs (`latin-caveat` 13.68%, `latin-great-vibes` 17.61% against a 12.50% tolerance). Widening to cover that means 26.4% at the 1.5x multiplier, more than double the slack on a guard that already admits "a defect smaller than an eighth of a case's ink passes". **So the tolerance stays at 12.50% and the baseline is pinned to the CI runner** - captured there via the new `update-export-render-baseline` workflow input, capture *refused* off-platform rather than left to discipline, and the guard **skips** on a developer machine with a message saying why. A green local `npm run test:e2e` therefore no longer covers this guard at all; only CI's does. **Linux is the pin because CI gates releases, explicitly not because it renders the way users do - there are no Linux users** (macOS, Windows, iPhone, Android; Linux is the build machine), which is what makes Artefact 2 an instrument defect rather than a fidelity target. **`স্ক` judged and confirmed a genuine divergence, plus two more found.** fontkit emits no `headlinebeng.*` component for it, so at 400px the conjunct is visibly missing the bar over its KA part and that part is a detached blob - judged by eye, not by a percentage - and its advance is 14% short (64.00 vs 74.50px), identical on both platforms. `preBaseVowel:ট+vowelSignI` had been passing on macOS by 0.7 points (13.78% against 14.48%) and fails cleanly once the rasteriser noise is gone. `conjunct:ddha` draws correctly but reports an 18%-short advance. All three added to `KNOWN_FONTKIT_DIVERGENCES` with measurements, taking it to six; the keep-or-drop decision was **re-opened as CLAUDE.md requires and re-taken as keep** (2.3% of 262 cases, in two named groups - retroflex component placement and conjunct assembly - against the 88% systemic disagreement that got Playpen Sans Hebrew dropped, and there is no better OFL Bengali face), and all six are now named to users in the Sign page's Bengali FAQ. **The list is at the size where a seventh should change the answer rather than extend it.** Full record, including the configurations tried and rejected: [docs/shaping-guard-platform-calibration.md](./docs/shaping-guard-platform-calibration.md) §5a-§5c. Original diagnosis retained below because it is what the evidence looked like before the two artefacts were separated. **Pickup brief, decision options and a ready agent prompt: [docs/shaping-guard-platform-calibration.md](./docs/shaping-guard-platform-calibration.md) — read it before touching any guard.** Treated as a release blocker because it is not one failing test: it means a green local `test:e2e` is not evidence about the exported PDF on the platform that actually gates releases, so every "shaping verified" claim on this board is scoped to whichever machine ran it. That undermines the guards as release gates, which is their entire purpose per Part II §6. Found on CI run [33242665616](https://github.com/shlomsh/pdkef/actions/runs/33242665616) (`b4ffd96`): Playwright reports 75 passed, 1 skipped, **5 failed**, but a full local macOS run of the same specs is 79 passed, 1 skipped, 1 failed. The one shared failure is SIGN-18. The other four - **Arabic (18 of 151 cases), Pashto (9 of 22), Bengali (2 of 259), and the 23-case exported-PDF render guard (2 drifted)** - do not reproduce locally at all, which is why the SIGN-04/SIGN-17 work was reported green: the focused Chromium set those passes were measured against runs on the author's macOS, and **macOS agreement is not evidence about the Linux runner**. The self-calibrated noise floor is the mechanism, and it moves in both directions between platforms: Arabic/Pashto calibrate to a floor of 2.76% on CI (tolerance 4.14%) where macOS sat far looser, while the Latin/GreatVibes guard calibrates to 69.02% on CI against 21.92% locally, and Tamil/Telugu are *tighter* on CI than locally (5.76% vs 8.49%, 6.53% vs 10.47%). So this is not "CI is noisier", it is that each platform's rasteriser produces a different floor and the guards were only ever calibrated against one of them. **Most of the failures look like sub-pixel placement, one does not.** The Arabic and Pashto cases carry pixel diffs of 4.16% to 24.74% against width deltas of 0.02px to 2.01px, which is the signature of antialiasing and rounding rather than a wrong glyph. The exception is Bengali `conjunct:ska "স্ক"` at `widthDiff=11.00px` (native 75.00 vs reconstructed 64.00) - an 11px disagreement on a 75px advance is a real shaping divergence, and it belongs in the same conversation as the three named entries in `KNOWN_FONTKIT_DIVERGENCES`, not in a tolerance. **Do not fix this by widening tolerances or regenerating baselines on whichever machine is handy** - the standing rule in SIGN-10 and SIGN-16 is that platform baseline differences require investigation. The first question to answer is whether the guards should calibrate per platform, pin a single rasterising platform for baselines, or run only on the runner they were calibrated against; the second is whether `স্ক` is a genuine fourth fontkit divergence. Until then CI stays red on `main` even with SIGN-18 fixed. **Both platforms measured 2026-08-29 on the same commit** (`b4ffd96`; macOS 15 / Darwin 25.5.0 local vs the `ubuntu-latest` runner), tolerance being `noise floor x 1.5`: Arabic **22.33% -> 4.14%** (18/151 fail), Pashto **22.33% -> 4.14%** (9/22), Bengali **14.48% -> 8.27%** (2/259), Tamil **12.74% -> 8.65%** (passes), Telugu **15.70% -> 9.80%** (passes), Latin/Pacifico **14.46% -> 45.30%**, Latin/DancingScript **29.61% -> 49.87%**, Latin/GreatVibes **32.88% -> 103.54%** (all pass). Export render guard: identical determinism floor of 0.00% and 12.50% tolerance on both, 0 drifted locally against 2 on CI, so its baselines are the platform-bound artefact rather than its calibration. **The asymmetry is the useful signal, and it argues against treating the macOS pass as the reference.** Linux is stricter on every script guard and looser on every Latin handwriting guard, so neither platform is simply "noisier" - but a 22.33% tolerance on Arabic is close to toothless (it admits a quarter of the pixels differing), which means the local green was weak evidence and the CI red is the more informative measurement. Whatever calibration policy is chosen, prefer the platform that yields the *tighter* floor per guard rather than the one that is convenient to run.
