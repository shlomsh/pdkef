# PE-03 · Fold finder for two pages to a sheet

*Opened 2026-11-04* from [the handoff](../../docs/scan-enhance-handoff.md), part of the page-enhancement plan. **Track: flatbed or copier scan of a book** (not phone photos; see the handoff's two tracks).

## Outcome

A book spread's spine is found as a thin slanted dark line and the sheet splits into two pieces. **Each piece is then a page of its own** (Shlomi, 2026-11-04, about flatbed scans of books: academic scans often squeeze two book pages onto one landscape sheet, and the better approach is to handle each page separately, as if they were different pages): the whole pipeline runs on each piece alone, not only a rotation: its own lean, then its own bend fit (rows and rules), per-line straightening where the fit leaves residue, its own squeeze toward the spine, its own cleanup, and the approach that suits that half (the left and right halves of one sheet can need different ones). Never one fit across both. The split is the first step of `planPage`, before anything is measured; every later step sees two ordinary pages.

## Acceptance

- [x] On the SCAN-36 book PDF's 8 pages the spine is found on 8/8 (tilt about 1.2 to 3.1 degrees), as in the prototype findings.
- [x] A single page finds no fold. An upright page never does, so a rule between two columns is not a spine; the sheet must already read across.
- [x] The finder is a pure function with a synthetic unit test.
- [ ] Each piece is scored on its own on the book with every approach tried (lean rotation, the shipped bend fit, per-line straightening); the plan keeps the best per piece, and the numbers go in this ticket.
- [ ] Nothing downstream measures across the spine: a test feeds a two-page sheet whose halves lean differently (the book's right halves lean -2.4 to -4.7 degrees, the left -1.4 to +0.3) and each half is straightened with its own lean.

## Measured 2026-11-04 (SCAN-19 follow-up; book PDF, 16 halves, median / worst per-line RMS in px, noise floor about 2)

| approach, each half on its own | median | worst |
|---|---|---|
| rotated only | 11.62 | 28.61 |
| the shipped bend fit on the half as is | 11.85 | 29.60 (flat on all 16: it sees no bend) |
| lean rotation | 2.03 | 2.24 |
| the shipped fit after the lean rotation | 2.01 | 2.20 (flat on 12 of 16) |
| the same, edge curves off | 1.99 | 2.20 |
| prototype per-line straightening (v3b) | 1.77 | 1.97 |

- The shipped fit on the unsplit sheet cannot do it: one near-linear shear across the whole sheet (page 5: 44 px,
  0.7 degrees), nothing at the spine, so the 1.8 to 3.5 degree difference between the two pages stays (page 0's
  right half stays at 28.65 to 29.60 px). The split is the fix, not the bend fit.
- After the split the lean rotation does the work; the bend fit adds nothing (2.03 to 2.01, inside the noise) and
  its edge-curve step misfires on 2 of 16 halves (it takes the image border or the spine shadow for a page edge:
  7R, 5L). Per-line straightening gains about 0.3 px: worth keeping only if it stays simple.
- The corners-only path insets 1.6% per edge and scales up 3.3%, which cuts outer-edge ink on a sparse page (7L:
  12.6% of its ink): a book half should not be inset like a photo.

## Production code on the book, 2026-11-04 (`findSpine` at 1000 px, then `pageLean`, `levelQuad`, `applyPiece`, inset 0)

- Spine on 8 of 8 sheets, tilt 1.0 to 3.0 degrees, about 30 ms a sheet.
- Rows after levelling: median 1.98 px, worst 2.28 px over 16 halves, on the noise floor (unlevelled: 11.52, 29.11).
- Leans read: left halves -1.35 to +0.28 degrees, right halves -4.55 to -2.30.
- Text lost: none. The most ink past a piece's edge is 1.05% (4L) and 0.82% (2R), and all of it is the copier's dark
  line along the sheet's border. Sparse 7L loses 0.01%, against 12.6% with the photo inset.
- Facing page pulled in: 0.12% at most (6R).
